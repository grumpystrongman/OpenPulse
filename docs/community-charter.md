# Community Charter

OpenPulse exists to make wearable and sensor interoperability easier to implement, easier to trust, and easier to adopt.

## Mission
Build an open standard and reference implementation that reduces repeated integration work across manufacturers, health systems, analytics teams, and software builders.

## What the community is trying to achieve
- make common wearable data easier to ingest and use
- preserve source provenance and manufacturer attribution
- support commercial adoption without locking participants into a single vendor stack
- keep the open core stable while allowing controlled extensions
- create a practical path from raw device payloads to analytics and clinical integration

## Why this matters now
The market does not need another vague interoperability project. It needs a working standard that people can run, inspect, test, and extend.

That is the bar OpenPulse is trying to meet.

## Principles
- Open core, clear governance.
- Backward compatibility by default.
- Plain and inspectable data contracts.
- Provenance on normalized facts.
- Manufacturer differentiation is preserved, not erased.
- Privacy, consent, and auditability are part of the design.
- Adoption matters more than theoretical completeness.

## Who this community serves
- manufacturers
- health systems
- digital health vendors
- analytics teams
- researchers
- AI and data platform teams

## What participation should feel like
Participants should be able to understand the standard quickly, try the software locally, identify gaps openly, and improve the ecosystem without fighting unnecessary process.

## What OpenPulse will avoid
- unnecessary standards complexity
- vendor-hostile normalization
- fake openness that hides key implementation details
- roadmap decisions that ignore implementation cost

## Bottom line
OpenPulse should be judged by whether it lowers real integration cost and increases real adoption. That is the standard the community should keep enforcing.
